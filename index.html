B.Sc. thesis

Final year research, MIST

Design and Development of a Low-Cost Anthropomorphic Prosthetic Hand with Hybrid EMG and Tactile Control

  • Developed a dual-mode prosthetic robotic arm controlled via EMG signals and a foot-switch based system.
  • Designed a user-driven calibration system with EMG threshold adjustment through a web interface.
  • Integrated a self-calibration mode for personalized control tuning based on individual muscle signals.
  • Focused on improving usability, adaptability, and accessibility of prosthetic control systems.
  • EMG Sensors
  • Web Interface
  • Embedded Systems
  • Tactile Control

This thesis focuses on the design and development of a low-cost, anthropomorphic prosthetic hand that combines EMG signal control with tactile feedback. The system is designed to be affordable and accessible for amputees in developing countries.

Key contributions

  • Hybrid control system: Integration of EMG-based gesture recognition with tactile control for redundant operation.
  • Calibration interface: Web-based EMG threshold calibration for personalized control tuning.
  • Tactile feedback: Incorporated pressure sensors for grip force feedback.
  • EMG control: Both three and five finger gripping supported.

Microfluidic Immunoassay for Quantitative Detection of Anti-dsDNA Antibodies in Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)

Designing and simulating a microfluidic biosensor for detection of anti-dsDNA autoantibodies, a key biomarker for Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE). Implementing computational analysis for therapeutic screening and binding affinity prediction.

  • Microfluidics
  • Biosensor
  • Immunoassay
  • CFD
  • Molecular Docking

Role: Research Assistant (RA), Department of Biomedical Engineering, Military Institute of Science and Technology (MIST).

Overview: Development of a lab-on-a-chip immunoassay platform for rapid, quantitative measurement of anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA) autoantibodies, a clinically established diagnostic and disease-activity biomarker for Systemic Lupus Erythematosus. The work combines microfluidic device design, computational fluid dynamics, surface-immobilisation chemistry and in silico therapeutic screening.

Key highlights

  • Device design: Microchannel and reaction-chamber geometry designed for controlled sample delivery and efficient antigen–antibody capture.
  • CFD simulation: Flow profile, shear stress and residence-time analysis to optimise mixing and analyte transport to the sensing surface.
  • Binding & capture modelling: Surface reaction kinetics and mass-transport modelling to predict capture efficiency and limit of detection.
  • Quantitative detection: Signal-response modelling for concentration-dependent quantification of anti-dsDNA antibody levels.
  • Computational therapeutic screening: Molecular docking and binding affinity prediction to evaluate candidate ligands and therapeutic molecules.
  • Clinical relevance: Aimed at low-cost, low-sample-volume, point-of-care SLE diagnosis and disease-activity monitoring.
